Forbidden Code: Survivor / Combined Rider and Deck Menu
Scope: design proposals for review, not an implemented screen rebuild.

Shared requirements
- One combined screen: player selection on the left, deck controls on the right.
- Deck building receives about 75% of the useful screen width.
- Character image becomes a small supporting element, approximately 160-210px high in a 1920x1080 mockup.
- Single quiet idle for a real-time model: an approximately 8-10 second breathing loop, with tiny chest/shoulder movement and planted feet. Remove attack previews, alternate-idle controls and automatic spinning from the normal menu. Drag rotation can remain optional for the 3D option.
- Portrait alternatives use detailed art in a calm resting pose. They do not imply a rigged or animated image; an animated portrait would need a separate treatment.
- Keep five riders, role/perk/tradeoff information, eight draft programs, two starters, saved presets, school filters, save/start actions, How To, Settings, leaderboard, Close and the compact Signal Dock.
- Do not change card rules or gameplay stats. Kinetic Cards remains 36 base damage / 0.85s cooldown.

Option A / Loadout Workbench
Small real-time-model-style view and compact rider controls on the left. A 4x2 card grid dominates the right. Two starter flags are applied directly to cards. Most direct deck-editing layout.

Option B / Deck Presets
Small detailed rectangular portrait on the left. Named deck presets, an eight-card deck strip, a compact library and selected-card inspector occupy the right. Best for choosing and adjusting saved builds; recommended portrait route.

Option C / Signal Console
Small circular portrait and slim rider list on the left. Organized spell rows with draft/starter toggles, a narrow inspector and an eight-slot deck ribbon dominate the right. Best for comparing numbers and editing quickly.

Render-quality assessment from current source
The current turntable allocates a 1000x800 render target and displays it in a 675x540 UI rectangle. It manually poses the model while the menu is paused and uses three point lights. A smaller display area can conceal visible mesh/shading limitations, but increasing render-target resolution alone cannot add missing geometry or texture detail. A separate menu material/normal/lighting review is appropriate before commissioning replacement Meshy models. An illustrated portrait can preserve approved concept-art detail without changing gameplay meshes.

Generated visual proposals are target mockups, not screenshots or proof of engine-render quality.
